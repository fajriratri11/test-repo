Project Story

My work follows a pipeline: first understand the system, then test it under uncertainty, and finally choose the best action. The paper on renewable energy and electricity demand shows why this matters: exploratory diagnostics and STL decomposition reveal temporal structure across nine energy datasets, while time-respecting splits and expanding-window validation support robust LSTM forecasting.

1

Sense the System

Forecasting and analytics reveal demand patterns, seasonal cycles, missingness, and anomalies so that the system can be understood before any decision is made.

2

Test Scenarios

Simulation and time-series decomposition make it possible to study alternative futures, compare regimes, and evaluate risk without disrupting the real system.

3

Choose the Best Action

Optimization and intelligent decision support convert insight into action, helping select robust strategies under uncertainty and operational constraints.

2024–2027

Forecasting Techniques for Growing Demand and Scalable Time-Series Methods

This project focuses on short-term and scalable forecasting with time-aware validation, lookback tuning, and robust normalization. The paper emphasizes that hourly forecasting with LSTM, combined with expanding-window cross-validation, is well-suited for energy time series with nonlinear dynamics and multi-scale dependence.

2024–2027

Stochastic Process Modelling for Generator Expansion Problem

This project treats generator expansion as a decision-making problem under uncertainty, where stochastic processes are used to model demand variability, operational risk, and investment timing. It connects naturally to optimization and intelligent decision support by turning uncertain future conditions into structured planning decisions.

Research Direction

From Forecasting to Intelligent Decision Support

My research combines predictive modeling, scenario analysis, and optimization into a single decision framework. I aim to create methods that are interpretable, reliable, and useful in industrial and engineering settings. The current paper’s empirical findings support this direction: solar follows a strong daily cycle, wind is more erratic and seasonal, and demand shows weekday/weekend plus climate-driven variation, which justifies a unified decision-support approach.

  • Forecasting and predictive analytics
  • Simulation modeling and scenario analysis
  • Optimization and operations research
  • Intelligent and data-driven decision systems
